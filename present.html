Roorkee · Chapter One · Origins

From a Small Village to an Engineering City

Roorkee & the Ganges Canal, 1860 — watercolour by William Simpson (archival) · Wikimedia Commons, public domain
1590s

On the Mughal record

Roorkee appears in the Ain-i-Akbari, the administrative gazetteer of Emperor Akbar's empire — already a recognised pargana.

Historical origins
1700s

The Landhaura estate

For a time part of the Landhaura state under Raja Ramdayal — before control passed to the East India Company.

Regional rule
1840s

A village of mud huts

Before 1840, Roorkee was still a small settlement — but it sat directly on the only practical canal line out of the Ganga at Haridwar.

Before the canal
1842

Headquarters of a giant

Canal works begin under Colonel Cautley. Workshops, engineers' bungalows, a cantonment and a college grow around the site within a decade.

Roorkee · Chapter Two · The Engineering Marvel

The Solani Aqueduct — Water Flows Over Water

1854

Canal opened after 12 years of construction — one of the largest canal systems on Earth at the time.

≈ 25 m

Canal bed held high above the Solani so the water never loses its gentle downhill slope.

~0.5 km

Length of the water-crossing — an elevated channel on piers and arches, built of brick & lime mortar.

170+ yrs

Still carrying canal water across the river today — working infrastructure, not a ruin.

Sources: Institution of Civil Engineers (ice.org.uk, “The Ganges Canal”); Ganges Canal records

Roorkee · Chapter Three · The Legacy

The Canal Built a College — and a Nation's Engineers

  • 22 Dec 1851

    India's first steam freight run

    A 'Jenny' locomotive hauls earth along a temporary construction railway from Roorkee to Piran Kaliyar — two years before India's first passenger train.

  • 1847 → 1854

    Roorkee College → Thomason College

    Founded beside the canal works to train engineers; renamed for James Thomason, the Lieutenant-Governor behind the canal.

  • 1850s

    The Bengal Sappers arrive

    The Bengal Engineer Group makes Roorkee its home — one of India's oldest cantonments, a military-engineering twin of the college.

  • 1949

    University of Roorkee

    The Thomason College becomes independent India's first engineering university.

  • 2001

    IIT Roorkee

    Elevated to India's seventh Indian Institute of Technology — the oldest engineering institution in the country, still growing.

Joshimath · Chapter Two · Ancient Heritage

Jyotirmath — the Monastery of Light

Narsingh Temple, Joshimath · photo: Rohit Sharma / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0
Joshimath in the 1890s — Kurt Boeck (archival) · public domain
8th century

One of Shankaracharya's four mathas

Adi Shankaracharya is credited with establishing four cardinal monasteries; Jyotirmath is the northern seat.

Narsingh Temple

The town's spiritual heart

Dedicated to Lord Narsingh, Vishnu's man-lion form — home to an idol that tradition traces back centuries.

Every winter

Badrinath's winter seat

When snow closes Badrinath (~Nov), the idol of Lord Badri is enthroned here; worship continues until the shrine reopens in spring.

Living culture

Garhwali life & the Kalpavriksha

Garhwali language, pilgrimage-season rhythms, and an ancient tree — the Kalpavriksha — linked to Shankaracharya by tradition.

Sources: Uttarakhand Tourism; Shri Badrinath–Kedarnath Temple Committee (badrinath-kedarnath.gov.in)

Joshimath · Chapter Three · Beyond the Town

One Valley, Many Journeys

Auli
~14 km · road / ropeway

Ski slopes & alpine meadows (bugyals); views of Nanda Devi. Winter–spring for snow.

Badrinath
~45 km · Char Dham

One of Hinduism's four Char Dham sites. Open roughly late April – early November.

Valley of Flowers
20 km + ~17 km trek

UNESCO World Heritage Site of alpine flora — drive to Govindghat, then trek. July–September.

Hemkund Sahib
trek via Ghangaria

Sikh shrine at ~4,630 m beside a glacial lake; shares the Valley of Flowers trail. Summer only.

Vishnuprayag
~10 km · river confluence

The Dhauliganga meets the Alaknanda — first of the great confluences beyond the town.

Tapovan
~15 km · across the river

Meadows and hot springs reached over the Dhauliganga — a quiet counterpoint to the pilgrim trail.

Distances approximate (road; treks one-way). Sources: Uttarakhand Tourism · UNESCO whc.unesco.org

Joshimath · Chapter Four · When the Mountains Send a Warning

A Town on an Old Landslide

1976

Mishra Committee warns

First official alarm: Joshimath stands on old landslide debris; recommends drainage control and limits on heavy construction. Warnings go largely unheeded.

2022

Satellites measure the sinking

ISRO/NRSC data: ~8.9 cm of subsidence Apr–Nov 2022, then ~5.4 cm in 12 days around New Year 2022–23.

2023

January crisis

Cracks spread through homes and roads — official surveys record ~868 damaged houses; families relocate as zones are declared.

2023+

Response — ₹1,658 crore

Centre approves a recovery plan on 'Build Back Better' principles; rehabilitation continues, and residents await permanent solutions.

2026

The debate continues

Aug 2026: tunnelling at the 520 MW Tapovan-Vishnugad project halts again after a cavity and water seepage (PTI).

Established: fragile geology + poor drainage + unplanned construction. Disputed: the hydropower project's role — government studies did not blame it; many residents and some experts disagree.
Sources: Mishra Committee (1976); ISRO/NRSC; NDMA (2023); Wadia Institute; PTI (Aug 2026); Sci. Reports & GRL 2024

The Comparison · One State, Two Philosophies

Two Cities, Two Relationships with Nature

Roorkee

Nature harnessed · the plains
  • Engineered the water — canalised the Ganga; canal opened 1854
  • Water above water — the Solani Aqueduct, still working today
  • Irrigation first — food security for millions across the Doab
  • Institutions from infrastructure — canal → college → IIT
vs

Joshimath

Nature respected · the Himalaya
  • Faith on a fragile slope — 8th-century matha at 1,890 m
  • Nature's calendar — winter seat of Badrinath; seasonal tourism
  • Fragile geology — a town on old landslide debris
  • The warning — subsidence shows development has limits
Development and nature must coexist — whether you engineer a river or worship a mountain.
